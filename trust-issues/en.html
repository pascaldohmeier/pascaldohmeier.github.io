One screen. One flag. A sign that says "just walk across :)". Don't believe it.

In Trust Issues you run and jump through a building made by the Architect — and he hates visitors. Every level has a nasty trick: floor that isn't floor, spikes that only appear when you land, a flag that runs away. The first time, you'll almost certainly fall for it. From the second time on, everything is the same and can be learned. After every level you clear, all of your attempts play back at once, in the graveyard.

Frequently asked questions

How do I control the game?

The game runs in landscape. The left half of the screen is for running: left of the gap between the arrow keys moves you left, right of it moves you right, and your thumb may slide between the keys. The right half is for jumping: a short tap is a small hop, holding gives a high jump. Running and jumping work at the same time. Pause is at the top left, restart at the top right. If you'd rather jump on the left, turn on "Swap controls" in the settings.

Who is the Architect?

He built the building, he puts up the wooden signs, and he comments on every one of your deaths. His signs lie. Mostly. From the third world on, some of them do tell the truth — then you have to decide whether to believe him this time.

Is the game unfair?

The traps are mean, the controls never are. Every level behaves exactly the same on every attempt; there is no randomness. Before a level goes into the game, a computer player checks that it can be beaten — with human reaction time, not just with pixel-perfect input. Wherever you've already died, a small red cross remains so you remember the spot.

How do lives work?

A world has 15 levels and is played in one go. You start with 15 lives; every death costs one, and so does the restart button. A level without a single death gives a life back, as does a real heart in the level — but most hearts are bait. When all lives are gone, the world starts over. Your progress in a world stays saved, even if you close the game.

What is the graveyard?

After every level you clear, all of your attempts play back at once as ghosts: the failed ones faded and with a cross at the end, the successful one bold. That way you see how often you fell for the same spot.

What are stars for?

Three per level: one for clearing it, one for clearing it without dying and one for a run under the target time. The game remembers your best result per level, plus your best time and how many times you've died in total.

What is expert mode?

Once you've cleared a world, its expert world opens: the same levels mirrored, with a new trap exactly where you've started to feel safe. There you only get 10 lives. Altogether that's 75 normal and 75 expert levels.

Ads?

Only when you're out of lives — and only if you want. In the "out of lives" window you can watch a video and get 5 lives, then you continue in the same level. This works at most three times per world run. There are no ads after a death, after a level or on restart, and no banners. Details in the privacy policy.

Can I buy anything?

No. Trust Issues has no in-app purchases.

Do I need an account? Internet?

No. The game runs entirely on your iPhone, even in airplane mode. You only need internet for the optional ad video.

Is the game available in English?

Not yet. Version 1.0 is German only, because the Architect's quips and signs are part of the game and deserve a good translation first.

Can I start over?

Yes: Settings → "Reset progress". This deletes progress, stars, best times and the death counter; your settings stay. It can't be undone.

Where is my data stored?

Only on your iPhone. See the privacy policy.
